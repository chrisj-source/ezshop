Production management

The questions you ask all day, answered on the screen

Nobody should have to walk the shop, call the parts desk and open a spreadsheet to find out where the week stands. The information is already in the system. It shows it to you.

What do I have in the shop?

The board, one screen, every car in the building with its stage, its age and who is on it. No walking the floor to find out.

What did we run this month?

Closed files book to a date you set, so the month's revenue, cycle times and counts are a report rather than a reconstruction.

How much do we need to collect?

Every file carries what was billed and what has been paid. Unpaid closed files are a list you can work down, not a surprise.

Twelve modules

Seven you're given. Five you choose.

Every module shares one set of customers, cars and jobs, so nothing is entered twice and nothing has to be kept in step by hand. There is no tier to climb out of — a module is either running in your shop or it isn't.

Automotive production management

What the modules actually do

Included

The board is the shop

Every open file sits in its real stage — teardown, PDR, body, paint, reassembly, QC, ready. Drag a car forward and everyone who needs to know is told. This is the production management half of the system, and it is the screen most of the shop lives on.

  • Collision, hail and combination shops each get their own lane and status set
  • Lanes and statuses are yours to name and reorder
  • Total losses sort to the top and turn red
  • Filter to one technician, or to the cars with nobody on them
  • Print exactly what is filtered on screen
Included

Leads, without a form to fill out

Somebody comes by for an evaluation or an estimate. You take their information once, at the counter, and the system tells you when it is time to reach back out.

  • Write the estimate on the lead at the counter, and the amount stays with it
  • A configurable stretch of silence flags the lead for chasing
  • Book the appointment from the lead itself
  • Won and lost are counted against what was quoted, not guessed at
Add when needed

Parts, from ordered to on the car

Lines grouped by what is happening to them — to order, on order, back order, received — with the order number, the vendor and the ETA on every one.

  • Late ETAs go red with the days they are late
  • A short shipment stays on order for what is still owed
  • Cost goes in when the invoice turns up, not before
  • Pull sheets print for ordering, for what is out, and for what came in
Included

Wholesale accounts that bill the way you quote

Trade work is not retail work. Wholesale customers keep their own rates, their own invoices and their own balance, and nothing about them has to be re-typed per job.

  • One client directory shared with the rest of the system
  • Descriptions are remembered; prices never are
  • Statements and aging per account
  • Wholesale records are kept as your business record, not aged out
Included

Insurance work with the claim on the file

Carrier, policy, claim number, deductible, date of loss, adjuster and estimator ride on the job. Import the estimate from CCC One or Mitchell and they fill themselves in, along with the parts lines and the labor hours.

  • Supplements tracked as their own checkpoints in each lane
  • Approved amounts overwrite the guess, and every change is recorded
  • Estimate written and estimate sent are separate states
  • Nothing is overwritten quietly — before and after are both kept
Add when needed

People find out without being told

A status change, a part arriving, a lead going quiet — the person who needs that fact gets it, and nobody else is copied for the sake of it.

  • Every event is a switch each person sets for themselves
  • Status changes can be scoped to only the cars you are on
  • Sends are capped so a busy morning is not fifty emails
  • Text messages are next, sharing the same wording
Included

Reporting you can stand behind

The numbers come off the same records the shop runs on, so a report is never a second set of books somebody has to keep in step.

  • Closed files keyed on the date you book them to
  • Cycle time in-to-ready, rental in-to-picked-up, A/R picked-up-to-paid
  • Parts margin per file and per line
  • Payroll and commission run off what the close-out actually settled

Access

Every permission is yours to set

There are no fixed job titles to squeeze your people into. You define the roles your shop actually has, name them what you call them, and tick exactly what each one may see and change.

Technicians get their own logins. A tech can be given the hours on a car without being shown what it bills, and can be limited to the cars assigned to them. Someone holding two roles gets the sum of both.

Roles you define
Owner and Technician are the only fixed ones, and you can rename both. Everything else is a row you name, tick and delete.
Technician logins
Every tech signs in as themselves. Their work, their hours, their cars.
Money is not one switch
File totals, parts cost and margin, and commission are three separate ticks. Labor hours is a fourth, separate from money entirely.
Only their own work
One tick per role narrows the board, the leads and the schedule to what that person is assigned.
Every change is recorded
Who changed what, when, and what it was before. Append-only, and nobody can edit it.

Questions

Before you ask

Can I add modules later?

Yes, and that is the point. Start with the seven every shop gets and turn on the others the month the work calls for them. Adding one does not move you to a different product or reset anything you have already entered, and turning one off again keeps the records it created.

How long does setup take?

It depends on the shop. A single-location shop with clean customer records is quick; a shop with years of history, several locations or a wholesale book to bring across takes longer. We will tell you what yours looks like after the demo rather than quote you a number now.

Does it work on a phone or tablet in the bay?

Yes. The board, the file, parts and check-in all work on a phone, and the sales screen is built for a phone on purpose. Technician logins work the same way, so a tech can see their own cars without walking to the office.

Who owns my data?

You do. The shop's data is only theirs, there is no cross over, and no one else has access to your customer and client information. Each shop has its own separate database.

What happens to old closed jobs?

A closed file is kept as a record. After a year it moves to archival storage, and personal details have a ten-year outer limit. The accounting shell — the RO number, the dates, the amounts and the labor — is never purged, because that is your business record. Wholesale and insurance client records are never purged.
